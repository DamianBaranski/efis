GPWS / TAWS design for this EFIS
================================

This is a light-aircraft terrain awareness design, in the shape of TSO-C151
Class B (no radio altimeter). It is not a certified GPWS. Phrases and the
caution-then-warning steps follow the Honeywell MK VI / MK VIII EGPWS pilot
guide and the Class B minimum set in TSO-C151b.

Sources
-------
- FAA TSO-C151b, Appendix 1 (Class A and Class B TAWS).
- Honeywell MK VI and MK VIII EGPWS Pilot Guide (060-4314).
- SKYbrary, Terrain Avoidance and Warning System.
- BendixKing KGP 560 / KGP 860 pilot notes (same caution / warning timing).

Three levels
------------
Every function uses the same three levels. A higher level of the same
function replaces the lower one. A higher-priority function replaces a
lower-priority one (see Priority).

  Advisory     Situational. Spoken once. No recovery required.
               Example: "Five hundred."  "Tower, two miles."

  Caution      The outer boundary. Amber. Change the path or the descent
               soon. Spoken once, then again only when the penetration
               grows, or on a fixed repeat while a look-ahead conflict
               remains.

  Warning      The inner boundary. Red. Immediate climb. "Pull up" repeats
               until the aircraft is back outside the inner boundary.

Same-function escalation (the 20 percent rule)
----------------------------------------------
Honeywell uses this on sink rate, too-low-terrain, and bank angle. Use it
here for every envelope that is not a look-ahead ribbon.

  1. First crossing of the outer boundary speaks the caution once.
  2. Each further 20 percent of penetration speaks it again.
     Penetration is how far the value has gone past the boundary
     (extra sink rate, extra altitude lost, extra bank, less clearance).
  3. Crossing the inner boundary replaces the caution with "Pull up",
     and that phrase repeats until the inner boundary is left.
  4. Leaving the outer boundary clears the alert. Re-arm only after a
     small hysteresis, so a value sitting on the line does not chatter.
     The navigation voice already does this for airspace and obstacles
     (re-arm a few tenths of a mile outside the gate).

Look-ahead terrain and obstacles use time instead of the 20 percent rule:

  Caution    about 40 to 60 seconds before the conflict.
             "Caution, terrain" or "Caution, obstacle", repeated about
             every 7 seconds while the conflict stays in the caution ribbon.
  Warning    about 30 seconds before the conflict.
             "Terrain, terrain, pull up" or "Obstacle, obstacle, pull up".
             "Pull up" continues until the path is clear.

TSO-C151b also fixes required terrain clearance (RTC) for the look-ahead
function. These are the clearances that should still exist at the caution,
not the height at which we start shouting:

  Phase        level flight    descending
  Enroute      700 ft          500 ft
  Terminal     350 ft          300 ft
  Approach     150 ft          100 ft
  Departure    100 ft          100 ft
               (do not alert if still more than 400 ft above the terrain)

On a normal approach inside about 1 NM of the threshold, shrink or inhibit
the look-ahead so a correct landing is quiet.

Priority
--------
One voice. The speaker already cuts off the current phrase when priority
is 1 or higher. Warnings use that. Cautions and advisories wait their turn
unless a warning is active, in which case they stay silent.

Class B internal order, from TSO-C151b Table 4-3, with obstacles added at
the same rank as terrain awareness (Honeywell treats them as one look-ahead):

  1  Sink rate pull-up                         warning, continuous
  2  Terrain or obstacle pull-up               warning, continuous
  3  Terrain or obstacle caution               caution, 7 s
  4  Too low terrain (premature descent,       caution
     clearance floor)
  5  Five hundred                              advisory, once
  6  Sink rate                                 caution, 20 percent
  7  Don't sink                                caution, 20 percent
  8  Bank angle                                caution, 20 percent

Navigation phrases (airspace, reporting points, nearest field, and the
existing obstacle distance call) sit below all of these. A GPWS caution
or warning suppresses them.

Height
------
There is no radio altimeter. Class B allows height above terrain as
barometric altitude minus the terrain cell, and the 500-foot call as
barometric height above the runway. This EFIS has GPS ellipsoid altitude.

  Height above a runway  =  GPS altitude − geoid − airports.csv elev_m
  Height above terrain   =  GPS altitude − terrain elevation in the mesh
  Obstacle top           =  obstacle ground elevation + obstacle height
  Clearance of an        =  GPS altitude − geoid − obstacle top
    obstacle

elev_m and the OpenAIP obstacle elevation are heights above mean sea level.
GPS altitude is above the ellipsoid. Over Czechia and southern Poland the
geoid offset is about 45 m. Apply it before any 500-foot or clearance test.
Do not subtract raw GPS altitude from elev_m.

Unknown obstacle height is drawn as a short mast (70 m wind, 40 m other).
Do not use that guess for a caution or a pull-up. Distance advisories may
still speak.

What exists today
-----------------
Published every frame:
  position, GPS altitude, pitch, roll, heading.
  Tablet also publishes ground speed and a vertical speed from successive
  GPS altitudes.
  Stratux reads BaroVerticalSpeed and GPSVerticalSpeed and then discards
  both. Ground speed is not taken from the situation message.

Already loaded, not used as an alert:
  Runway catalog: threshold position, heading, length, elevation.
  Obstacle catalog: position, ground elevation, height above ground, kind.
    The public nearby list currently exports height only. Ground elevation
    is already parsed and is used to place the mast.
  Terrain mesh: elevation is in the loaded triangles. Nothing samples it.

Obstacle voice today (src/voice/nav_voice.cpp) is an advisory, not a GPWS
warning. It picks the nearest relevant obstacle inside 3 NM (wind, chimney,
tower, or anything 50 m or taller). Wind farms inside 1.5 km collapse to
one phrase. It speaks at 2 NM, and again overhead for a single obstacle
that is not a turbine. Both phrases are priority 0. It never reads aircraft
altitude, vertical speed, or track, so a turbine level with the aircraft
and a turbine 3000 ft below sound the same.

Now
---
These can be built from data the process already holds. No new sensor.
Stratux sink-rate needs the two vertical-speed fields stored; the poll
already receives them.

1. Obstacle alert, on top of the distance advisory.

   The catalog is enough. No terrain sample.

   Advisory (already spoken)
     "Tower, two miles." / "Over chimney, 80 metres."
     Horizontal only. Keep it. It does not escalate into a pull-up.

   Caution
     The track, ground speed, and vertical speed reach an obstacle top
     inside about 60 seconds, or the aircraft is inside a horizontal
     radius of the obstacle with clearance under a margin (start near
     300 ft, tighter near a runway).
     "Caution, obstacle." Repeat about every 7 seconds.
     Deeper loss of clearance retriggers on the 20 percent rule when the
     aircraft is already beside the obstacle rather than looking ahead.

   Warning
     The same geometry inside about 30 seconds, or clearance through the
     obstacle top along the track.
     "Obstacle, obstacle, pull up." Repeats until the path clears the top
     by the caution margin.
     Skip this level when height is unknown.

2. Bank angle (Mode 6).

   Roll is live on both sources.

   Advisory: none.
   Caution: "Bank angle, bank angle" once when roll exceeds the limit.
   Warning: none. This function has no pull-up.

   Escalation from the EGPWS guide, simplified for no radio altitude:
     First call at the limit.
     Another call at 20 percent more bank.
     Continuous call beyond the second 20 percent step.
     Re-arm when roll drops back under the first limit.

   Without height above terrain, use one GA limit around 45 to 50 degrees.
   When height above the nearest runway is known and low, tighten the
   limit (EGPWS turboprop: about 50 degrees above 210 ft, steeper down
   toward 15 degrees at very low height, inhibited below about 10 ft).

3. Five hundred (Class B required callout).

   Advisory only. Once per descent through 500 ft above the nearest
   runway elevation, while descending and aligned with that runway.
   "Five hundred."
   Away from a runway this call waits on a terrain sample (see Later).

4. Excessive descent rate (Mode 1). Class B required.

   Height is height above the nearest runway while inside the airport
   area. Enroute sink rate waits on terrain height.

   Caution, outer boundary: "Sink rate."
     Again on each extra 20 percent of sink past that boundary.
   Warning, inner boundary: "Pull up", continuous, until the sink rate
     is back outside the inner curve.

   The curves are sink rate against height. A steeper descent is allowed
   when higher. Class B extends the envelope above the old 2500 ft radio-
   altitude cap. Numbers should be taken from RTCA/DO-161A as widened in
   TSO-C151b Appendix 3, not invented ad hoc. Until those curves are
   copied in, a temporary pair is enough to wire the voice:

     outer  at 500 ft AAL, descent faster than about 1500 ft/min
     inner  at 500 ft AAL, descent faster than about 2500 ft/min
     both boundaries open up as height increases

5. Premature descent (PDA). Class B required. Caution only.

   Below a nominal 3 degree path to the nearest runway threshold, using
   threshold elevation. Normal circuit heights must stay quiet. TSO asks
   that VFR in the airport area and a circling approach not alert.

   "Too low, terrain."
   Again only after another 20 percent of altitude below the path.
   No pull-up for this function (TSO Table 4-1: warning not required).

6. Terrain clearance floor near a runway. Caution only.

   A floor that rises with distance from the nearest runway, compared
   with GPS altitude minus field elevation. Same phrase and the same
   20 percent repeat as premature descent. This is the airport-area
   version. The enroute floor needs the mesh.

7. Don't sink (Mode 3). Class B required. Caution only. Partial.

   Arm when the aircraft climbs away from a runway threshold (low height
   above that field, then a positive climb). Alert on a following loss
   of altitude.

   "Don't sink."
   If the loss continues into the clearance floor: "Too low, terrain."
   Again on each extra 20 percent of altitude lost.
   No pull-up.
   Without gear and flap positions the takeoff and go-around state is
   only geometric, so this will miss a go-around that never gets near
   the threshold elevation, and it can false-arm on a low pass.

Later (terrain height query)
----------------------------
The loaded .btg tiles already contain the ground elevation. These functions
turn on when a lat/lon sample of that mesh exists. Obstacle warning does
not wait on this.

8. Look-ahead terrain (FLTA). The main Class B function.

   Project position with track (or heading), ground speed, and vertical
   speed. Turn rate from successive headings should widen the ribbon.
   Two tests, both with the RTC table above:

     Reduced clearance: still above the terrain, but the projected
     clearance is below the RTC for the phase of flight.
     Imminent impact: the projected path meets terrain.

   Caution then warning, 60 seconds then 30 seconds, as in the look-ahead
   timing section. Phrase is "terrain", not "obstacle".

9. Excessive terrain closure (Mode 2).

   Classic Mode 2 is radio-altitude rate. The geometric stand-in is the
   rate of change of height above the mesh under the aircraft.

   Caution: "Terrain, terrain."
   Warning: "Pull up", continuous.
   Class A splits this by flap position (2A / 2B). We have no flap
   discrete, so one envelope, inhibited on a stable approach inside
   1 NM of the threshold.

10. Five hundred above terrain, and the enroute clearance floor.

    The same two phrases as items 3 and 6, using mesh elevation when no
    runway is the reference. TSO wants the 500-foot call above terrain
    specifically when the aircraft is not in the airport or PDA area.

Not available
-------------
  Too low gear, too low flaps (Mode 4A / 4B). No gear or flap input.
  Glideslope (Mode 5). No ILS or glidepath deviation. Premature descent
    is the substitute, and it does not say "Glideslope".
  Windshear (Mode 7). No air-data, angle of attack, or predictive feed.
    The accelerometer is not a windshear sensor.
  Radio-altitude Mode 1 and Mode 2 as written in DO-161A. The geometric
    versions above are the Class B form, not a radio-altimeter GPWS.
  Certified Class A. That also requires a radio altimeter, landing
    configuration, and a terrain display driven with caution and warning
    colors. The 3D terrain view can later paint the conflicting cell
    amber then red. It is not required for Class B.

Voice wiring
------------
Warnings call the announcer at priority 1 so they cut off navigation
speech. Cautions use a lower priority and the 7-second or 20-percent
gate so they do not pile up. The existing obstacle distance phrase stays
at priority 0 and must not speak while a caution or warning for that
same obstacle is active.

Each function keeps a small memory: armed, level, and the penetration
at the last utterance. That is the same pattern as the airspace and
obstacle memory already in NavVoice.
